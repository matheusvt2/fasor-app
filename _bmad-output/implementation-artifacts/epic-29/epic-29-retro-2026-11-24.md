## Findings

1. **Investigation first worked, and the cause was found in the code, not guessed.** #372's body has a before table per screen and overlay kind (tap outside, Escape, device back, in-app back) and four proven causes: no overlay owned a history entry, so the device back did a router pop (from the Sumario that lands on Home, Bruno's "volta para o inicio de tudo"); the Export dialog's close replaced its `?exportar=1` entry and left two Sumario entries; `DialogShell` returned focus without `preventScroll`; the browser restores scroll before Home has drawn from IndexedDB. The story's DoD asked for the before and after tables in the PR: both are there. [accept]
2. **The first design failed in the batch and was replaced.** Closing an overlay by taking its entry back with `history.back()` raced navigations started by the overlay's action (for example "Abrir primeira ficha" after its IndexedDB write); the first gate had 3 of 7847 unit reds and 7 e2e reds (#372 body, "Gate"). The batch replaced it with the released-entry design (`state/overlay-history.ts`) before its PR. [accept]
3. **The history layer took three rounds after its first design.** #374 (S1: nested overlays closed without back left one back doing nothing; S2: a forward step dead across a released entry; S3b: "Ver no sumario" left two Sumario entries; each reproduced red first) gave every entry a `__position`. #377 (S2 and N2 of the pass) made the Sumario keep its view under its history entry's router key and made the in-app "Voltar" step back in history when the address it leads to is the one the entry was pushed from. The layer is now `overlay-history.ts` (241 lines, +304/-63), `history-return.ts` (107, new) and `scroll-restoration.ts` (77, new), with `e2e/overlay-back.spec.ts` (395 lines) and `overlay-history.test.tsx` (335) over it, and it wraps `pushState` and `replaceState` once to follow the router (#372 body). The rounds found real defects each time, with a failing test first; they also say that a navigation layer under a router gets its invariants from use, which the two gates and one review did find. [accept; the residual gap is the next finding]
4. **Coverage is a hand-kept list.** The hook is used by `components/dialog-shell.tsx`, `components/overflow-menu.tsx`, `components/tabs.tsx`, `surfaces/ficha/camera-dialog.tsx`, `surfaces/papeis/papeis-grid.tsx` and `surfaces/app-shell.tsx` (grep of `overlay-history`). `apps/web/src/surfaces/points/point-link-picker.tsx` opens a React Aria `Select` `Popover` and is not among them; #372 says the combobox list "was not changed" because it is non-modal, and says nothing of this Select. Whether the device back on it leaves the screen was not tested. Also open: a Sumario that was the tab's first page keeps no view on a back, because the router keeps it under its shared "default" key (`state/history-return.ts`, `UNKEYED`; `deferred-work.md`). [defer: A2, A3]
5. **The behavior of the in-app "Voltar" changed in a fix batch, not in a story.** #372 read "go back twice" as the device back and kept the in-app "Voltar" as a hierarchy move (Sumario to Project, ficha to Sumario with the row focused). #377's N2 changed it for a ficha opened from the Sumario: it now steps back in history. The batch changed two specs on purpose (`typed-values-kept` 25.2-E2E-001 and 25.2-E2E-002 return to the sheet with a forward step, #377 body). The change is recorded as dated lines under Story 29.2 in `epics.md`, so it is traceable, and it is open reading 7 for Matheus (`epic-28-context.md`). [accept the record; decision: E28-A1]
6. **The evidence is Chromium emulation.** The investigation ran Playwright Chromium with touch, Pixel 7 emulation (412 px) and a 768 x 1024 tablet; there is no installed-app display mode (the app has no web manifest, `source-deltas.md`), and a download or new tab was checked from code, not run, because it needs LibreOffice generation (#372 body). A real Android or iPad device was not checked in the pass or in #377 ("Not verified"). Bruno's complaint came from his own device, so his confirmation is the real acceptance test and is not recorded. [defer: A1, A2]
7. **29.1 is complete and guarded.** `POST /api/auth/change-password` joined `AUTH_ROUTES` (it had been a 404 since Story 20.13), goes through the get-session guard (`401 user_inactive` for a paused or removed user) and two limits that reuse the Story 26.6 rules (per address 20/min, per user 5/min), and forces `revokeOtherSessions: true`; the web dialog reuses the kernel's length rule and `copy.setPassword` messages instead of a second copy (#372 body). The review's worry about a non-JSON body is not reachable: the origin guard (Story 20.13) answers 403, and `29.1-API` now asserts form-encoded, multipart and text bodies get 403 with the password and sessions unchanged (#374 body). The offline case is covered by `e2e/change-password.spec.ts:83` (`setOffline(true)`, disabled with its reason); the hands-on pass lists "change password offline" as not checked, which is the pass, not the suite. Eight authored strings are listed for Bruno in #372. [accept]
8. **Aggregate views, moderate depth.** Duplication: none found in the Epic 29 files; the dialog and the limits reuse existing rules (finding 7). Size: `change-password-dialog.tsx` 219 lines new; no existing large file grew by more than the Epic 28 table shows. Ownership: the change-password rule and messages come from the kernel and `copy`; the history layer is web-only state with no domain logic. Architecture delta: not derived with a tool (narrowed). Spec to implementation: both stories' acceptance criteria and DoD lines are met in the PR bodies (api integration, `@p0` specs `29.1-E2E-001` and `29.2-E2E-001a` to `d`, `002a`, `002b`, mocks amended in `90-account.html` with a `MOCK-GUIDE.md` note, KB and `log.md`). The one reading the story left open ("go back twice") is finding 5. [accept]
