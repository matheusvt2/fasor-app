C9: verified in part. Independent source: The Pump & Motor Works, "Understanding IEEE 43 and Motor Insulation Resistance Testing" (https://www.pmwus.com/understanding-ieee-43-and-motor-insulation-resistance-testing-2/, PMW, n.d., accessed 2026-09-18). Evidence: "insulation resistance should be at least 1 megaohm per kV of rated voltage plus 1 megaohm", presented in the context of motors and IEEE 43. Notes: this source does not state the 40 °C reference and does not call the rule arbitrary; the Megger "arbitrary" statement was not independently checked. A web-search summary (source not identified, so not usable as evidence) says IEEE 43-2000 applies kV+1 only to windings made before about 1970, field windings and others not otherwise described. It gives 100 MΩ for most form-wound AC/DC armature windings after 1970 and 5 MΩ for random-wound and <1 kV windings. If that is right, kV+1 is the lowest of three category minimums, not the general IEEE 43 minimum. Check the IEEE 43 Table 3 wording before stating "Rm = kV+1" without qualification.
